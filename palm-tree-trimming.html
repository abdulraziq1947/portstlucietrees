Palm Tree Trimming & Removal

Palms are not shade trees. A queen palm or a sabal palm drops heavy fronds and seed, and a dead one snaps in wind instead of slowly rotting. Trim the crown, or take the palm out when the trunk is done.

Call 772-492-4436

When to call

  • Brown fronds are hanging over the roof, the pool, or the walk.
  • Seed pods and boots are piling up and holding moisture.
  • The crown is yellow, the spear is loose, or the trunk is soft.
  • The palm is in the way of a new roof, a fence, or a driveway.

How the work goes

  1. Identify the palm and look at how close the crown is to the house.
  2. Remove dead fronds and loose boots. Green fronds that the palm still needs stay on.
  3. If the palm is dying or in the way, cut the trunk in sections and lower it.
  4. Haul the debris. Palm waste is heavy and does not belong in a normal trash can.

What changes the price

  • Height of the crown and whether a climb or a lift is required.
  • How many palms are on the property.
  • Removal versus a trim.
  • Haul-away, and stump or root ball if the palm comes out.

Questions about this job

How often should a palm be trimmed?

Dead fronds can come off when they hang. Skinning a palm down to a few green fronds hurts the tree and is a common reason crowns fail in wind. A light cleanup once or twice a year is enough for most yard palms.

Can you remove a palm close to the house?

Yes. The trunk is cut in pieces and lowered, the same way a hardwood is handled when there is no room to drop it. Tell us if the palm is against the eaves or over a pool cage.

Is a leaning palm dangerous?

A slight lean can be normal. A new lean, a soft trunk, or a loose crown is not. Send a photo. If the spear pulls out in your hand, the palm is dying and should come down before a storm.

Do palm removals need a permit in Port St. Lucie?

A native palm with at least 10 feet of clear trunk can be a protected tree inside the city and may need a permit. Many queen palms people plant in yards are a different case. We will tell you what is in the yard, and the city or your HOA has the final say.
