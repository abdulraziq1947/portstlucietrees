Land & Lot Clearing

A lot that has grown up in brush, saplings, and vines is not ready to fence or build on. Clearing means taking that growth down and getting the debris off the property, not just cutting a path.

Call 772-492-4436

When to call

  • You bought a lot and cannot see the corners.
  • Brush is growing into the house or the fence line.
  • Small trees have filled an area you want to use.
  • A contractor needs the vegetation gone before they start.

How the work goes

  1. Walk the lot and mark trees you want to keep.
  2. Cut brush and unwanted trees and keep equipment off septic and drain fields.
  3. Haul or chip the debris.
  4. Leave stumps called out in the estimate. Grinding is a separate step if you need the ground smooth.

What changes the price

  • How thick the growth is and how large the area is.
  • Trees you are keeping, which slow the crew down on purpose.
  • Haul-away distance and volume.
  • Stumps, and any permit for protected trees inside a city.

Questions about this job

Will you clear a lot down to bare sand?

We remove the vegetation we were hired to cut. Grading, fill, and a finished pad are not tree work. If you need the ground flat for a builder, say so before we price the stumps.

Can you save the oaks and take the brush?

Yes. Mark the trees that stay. Clearing around keepers takes more care than cutting everything, and the price reflects that.

Do I need a permit to clear?

Inside Port St. Lucie, protected trees on the lot can require a removal permit even on a vacant parcel. Brush and saplings are a different category. We will point out the larger trunks. You should check with the city before those come down. City of Port St. Lucie tree rules.

What happens to the debris?

It is chipped or hauled. We do not leave a pile at the curb unless you asked for that and the city allows it.
