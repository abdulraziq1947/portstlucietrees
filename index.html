Questions before you call

How much does tree removal cost in Port St. Lucie?

There is no flat price. The estimate depends on trunk size, how close the tree is to the house, whether a truck can reach the yard, and whether the wood and stump leave with the crew. A small palm in an open yard is a different job from a large oak over a roof. Call with the address and a photo and we will tell you what has to be included.

Can you remove a tree close to my house?

Yes, when the tree can be taken down in pieces instead of felled in one cut. The crew starts at the top, lowers limbs away from the roof, and keeps the trunk off the walls. Tight access takes more time, so a photo of the tree and the house is the useful first step.

Do you offer emergency tree removal after storms?

Yes. After wind we cut trees that are on a house, across a driveway, or hung up in another tree. We do not cut a tree that is in a power line, and we do not promise an arrival time we cannot keep. Call 772-492-4436, say what fell, and we will schedule the next crew that can take it.

Does homeowners insurance cover fallen tree removal?

It depends on your policy and what the tree hit. A windstorm that drops a tree onto a covered structure is often covered so the tree can be moved to make repairs, while a tree that only falls in the yard is often not covered. Debris removal usually has a dollar limit, and a hurricane deductible can apply. Ask your insurer before you assume the bill is paid.

Do I need a permit to remove a tree?

In the City of Port St. Lucie, a protected tree is generally a trunk of 12 inches or more at chest height, or a native palm with at least 10 feet of clear trunk. Those trees need a permit from Planning and Zoning, and cutting one without a permit can mean a fine and a replacement tree. Other cities and counties use different rules. We will say what we see on site, and you should confirm the permit with the city or your HOA before a protected tree comes down. City of Port St. Lucie tree rules.

Can you remove the stump after cutting down a tree?

Yes. The stump can be ground on the same visit or on a return trip if the yard is too wet or too tight for the grinder. Grinding takes the stump below the surface so it is no longer in the way of a mower. Roots farther from the trunk may stay in the soil and settle over time.
